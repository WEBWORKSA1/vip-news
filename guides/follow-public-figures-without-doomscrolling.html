How to follow public figures without doomscrolling

Build a short watchlist, give it a schedule, and let the rest go.

3 minute read. Written for Vip.News.

Following the people who shape your industry, your investments or your interests is useful. Following everyone, all day, is not. A watchlist turns an endless feed into a short, finite read.

Choose fewer people than you think

Pick five to ten names whose decisions affect you: the head of the central bank, the chief executive of your largest holding, the minister for your sector, an artist or athlete you care about. If a name has not produced a decision you acted on in a year, remove it.

Follow decisions, not commentary

For each person, identify where decisions are announced: filings, official statements, press conferences, match days, release dates. Read those. Skip the reaction cycle that follows, which mostly repeats the announcement with opinions attached.

Give it a time and a limit

Check once or twice a day at set times. Use the search box on our latest headlines page to see today's coverage of any name, then stop when you reach the end of the list.

Notice how you feel afterward

If a source regularly leaves you agitated and no better informed, it is optimised for engagement, not for you. Replace it with a calmer one that reports the same facts.
