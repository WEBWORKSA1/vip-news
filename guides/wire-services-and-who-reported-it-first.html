Wire services, outlets and aggregators: who actually reported it

Why the same sentence appears on fifty sites, and how to tell original reporting from repetition.

4 minute read. Written for Vip.News.

Search for a big story and the same paragraph turns up on dozens of websites. That is not copying in the usual sense. It is how the news supply chain works, and knowing the chain helps you judge how solid a story is.

Wire services

Agencies such as the Associated Press, Reuters and Agence France-Presse employ reporters around the world and license their work to other publishers. A regional newspaper cannot staff a bureau in every capital, so it buys the wire. When you see an agency credit at the top of an article, the reporting was done by the agency.

Original outlets

Newsrooms add their own reporting: exclusives, investigations, interviews and analysis. Signs of original work are named reporters, a dateline showing where the reporter was, documents the outlet obtained and quotes given directly to it.

Aggregators

Aggregators collect headlines from many publishers and link to them. A good aggregator saves time and shows the range of coverage; it does not replace the article it points to. Vip.News is an aggregator for headlines: every headline links to the publisher that wrote the story, and we do not copy article text.

Why this matters when you read

  • Fifty sites running one agency report is one source, repeated.
  • Two outlets confirming a fact independently is stronger than fifty repeats.
  • Phrases like "according to reports" mean the outlet has not confirmed the claim itself.

When a story matters to you, follow the credit line back to whoever did the reporting and read it there.
